The small business playbook

A better website starts with
a clearer customer journey.

Your website, landing pages, SEO and social media shouldn’t feel like separate projects. Here’s how to help them work together for your business.

A professional website isn’t just a place to put your logo. It should help a visitor answer three questions: am I in the right place, can this business help me, and what should I do next?

For a small business, a clear experience is often more useful than a complicated one. Before adding another feature or publishing another post, look at the path your customers take—from discovering you to getting in touch.

01. Make your offer clear before you make it clever.

Your first screen should explain what you do, who it is for and how to take the next step. Visitors shouldn’t have to decode a slogan to understand your services.

  • Use a headline that describes your service in everyday language.
  • Add a short explanation of the problem you help solve.
  • Choose one primary action, such as “Request a quote” or “Discuss my project.”
  • Support your offer with accurate information, real work and genuine testimonials where available.
A useful test: Show your homepage to someone unfamiliar with your business. Can they explain what you offer after a quick look?

02. Choose the right format for the job.

A landing page and a business website can both be useful, but they solve different problems. Choose the format around your audience, content and goals—not simply the number of pages.

Business website

A good fit when visitors need to explore several services, understand your process, learn about your business and find contact details in one place.

Focused landing page

A good fit for a specific offer, campaign or service. Keep the message focused, answer key questions and guide visitors towards one main action.

Whichever you choose, plan the page content before design begins. Clear service descriptions and useful FAQs make the experience easier to build—and easier to understand.

03. Design for the phone in your customer’s hand.

A layout that looks polished on a large screen can still feel awkward on a phone. Mobile-first design means paying attention to readable text, comfortable tap targets and a sensible content order—not simply shrinking the desktop version.

  • Keep headings readable without horizontal scrolling.
  • Make buttons and links easy to tap.
  • Use properly sized images and avoid unnecessary heavy effects.
  • Check that fixed contact buttons don’t cover important content.
  • Test forms with the on-screen keyboard open.

Review the site on real devices where possible. Browser resizing helps, but it doesn’t reveal every issue with touch interaction, keyboards or loading conditions.

04. Give search engines useful, accurate content.

SEO starts with helping people understand your services. Use descriptive page titles, logical headings and original content that answers the questions your customers actually ask.

Give important services enough detail, link related pages together and keep contact information consistent. Technical checks also matter: make sure pages load correctly, important content is accessible and pages you want found are not marked noindex.

Keep expectations realistic: SEO is ongoing work. A well-structured website can help search engines understand your content, but no honest provider can guarantee a particular ranking or a fixed number of enquiries.

05. Let social media lead somewhere useful.

Consistent social content can introduce your business, explain your services and answer common questions. The next step should feel connected to the post—not like arriving at an unrelated website.

  • Match the offer and wording between your post and its destination page.
  • Keep your business name, visuals and contact details consistent.
  • Plan a mix of helpful guidance, service explanations and genuine business updates.
  • Use a realistic publishing schedule you can maintain.

A clear content calendar helps you show up with purpose. It is more useful than posting just to fill a gap.

06. Make it easy to start a conversation.

Your contact form should ask for enough detail to understand the enquiry without creating unnecessary work. A name, contact information, service selection and short message are often a practical starting point.

Show a clear sending state and an honest success or error message. Explain how submitted details are used in your privacy notice. If you offer phone or WhatsApp contact, make sure those links open the correct destination.

Most importantly, submit a real test enquiry before launch and confirm that it reaches the correct inbox. A form that looks complete is not the same as a verified delivery setup.

Before you share your website

  • Your offer and main action are clear.
  • Pages work on mobile, tablet and desktop.
  • Images load and links go to the right place.
  • Your form has been tested end to end.
  • Page titles and sharing information use your own branding.
  • Claims, portfolio examples and testimonials are accurate.
  • Your privacy notice reflects the services your site uses.
